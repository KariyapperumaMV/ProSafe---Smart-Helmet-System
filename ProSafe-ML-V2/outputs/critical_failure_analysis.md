* W_002's most frequent Critical signature is `uv + uv_crit + hr` (88% of its Critical rows); it occurs in only **24 training rows** (synthetic + W_001), labelled Safe 0, Warning 12, Critical 12.
* Share of seconds labelled Critical when the UV-critical counter is active: synthetic 5%, W_001 30%, W_002 24%. The synthetic data maps critical UV to Warning far more often than the real workers do.
* Critical episodes: synthetic 7 (median 60 s), W_001 17 (median 47 s), W_002 3 (median 33 s) - tens of independent events, not thousands.

## 3. Conclusion

* Training Critical is driven by long **heat exposure and heart-rate / body-temperature escalation**. W_002's Critical is driven by **sustained critical UV with moderately raised heart rate and no heat**. That mechanism is almost absent from every training set, so no training-side search can reward detecting it: missed W_002 Critical seconds land in Warning (rarely Safe). This is a training-data coverage problem, not an algorithm or tuning failure.
* Exposure-duration features encode *which* hazards co-occurred with Critical in training. They raise overall accuracy but specialize models to the training mechanisms.
* Next data step (not done here): collect / simulate UV-, gas- and noise-driven Critical episodes without heat, and validate on a new unseen real worker. Until then deterministic critical-exposure rules should run alongside the classifier.
